About the Network

An international, open and collaborative community for early-career researchers working on Biological Nitrification Inhibition and closely related subjects.

Why we started

The network grew out of the 5th International BNI Consortium Meeting in Japan in 2024, where very few early-career researchers were present. Conversations there highlighted the need to connect PhD students and other early-career researchers in the field, to give them opportunities, and to build shared practices, reproducibility and peer support.

The BNI-ECR Network was founded and is coordinated by Gabriel S. Rocha, a PhD candidate in Microbial Ecology at the Netherlands Institute of Ecology (NIOO-KNAW). Its kick-off meeting took place on 12 November 2025.

Our mission

To foster an international, open and collaborative environment for early-career researchers working on BNI-related topics.

An independent, ECR-led community. The network is distinct from the International BNI Consortium. Affiliations listed on this site belong to individual members and do not imply institutional ownership, sponsorship or endorsement.

Core values

Collaboration over competition

Openness and inclusivity

Transparency and reproducibility

Community-driven growth

Our objectives

  1. 01Connect early-career researchers working on BNI across countries, institutions and disciplines.
  2. 02Create opportunities for members to present and discuss their research.
  3. 03Promote exchange of protocols, methods, experiences and troubleshooting knowledge.
  4. 04Encourage interdisciplinary and international collaboration.
  5. 05Develop joint writing projects, including perspectives, reviews and research proposals.
  6. 06Give early-career researchers greater visibility within the international BNI community.
  7. 07Build connections between early-career researchers and established BNI researchers.
  8. 08Support the professional development of researchers entering the BNI field.
  9. 09Help identify shared research priorities, methodological challenges and knowledge gaps.
  10. 10Maintain an active next generation of researchers contributing to BNI science and application.

Who can join

Membership is free and intended primarily for:

  • PhD candidates
  • Postdoctoral researchers
  • Researchers within approximately five years of completing their degree
  • Researchers whose work is directly connected to BNI or its application

Senior BNI researchers who would like to support early-career activities are also welcome to get in touch.
